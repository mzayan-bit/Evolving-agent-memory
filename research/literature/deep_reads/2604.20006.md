# Citation

**From Recall to Forgetting: Benchmarking Long-Term Memory for Personalized Agents**. Uddin, Md Nayem; Shubham, Kumar; Blanco, Eduardo; Baral, Chitta; Wang, Gengyu. First public: 2026-04-21; Author-reported status: Accepted to ACL 2026 Findings. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20006v1); v1 (2026-04-21).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Evaluate remembering and forgetting evolving personal information

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Evaluate remembering and forgetting evolving personal information. Its scope matters because simulated personalities and llm judges limit external validity.

## Method

Generate persona histories with facts created, mutated and deleted across sessions. Test remembering, reasoning and recommendation using criteria that score required information and the absence of invalid information.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Evolving persona information
- **Retrieve:** Downstream memory use
- **Update:** Mutations across sessions
- **Consolidate:** Not isolated as a memory operator
- **Invalidate:** Outdated information must cease influencing answers
- **Forget:** FAMA explicitly scores forgetting-aware behavior

## Representation

Multi-session evolving personas

## Controller / Decision Policy

Remember, reason and recommend tasks; FAMA. Multi-session ingestion and three-judge evaluation; moderate API burden even with a small persona count.

## Datasets

Memora: ten personas and weekly/monthly/quarterly evolution schedules.

## Baselines

Personalized memory agents and long-context/retrieval approaches in the paper.

## Metrics

FAMA task criteria, with majority decisions from GPT-4.1, Haiku-4.5 and Gemini-2.5 judges.

## Main Findings

Forgetting correctness is already evaluated separately from storage capacity. Error analysis finds outdated personalization in 16 of 25 sampled recommendation failures; this is not a prevalence estimate over the entire benchmark.

## Ablations

Task and time-scale comparisons; qualitative taxonomy of 75 selected incorrect cases.

## What It Actually Solves

Evaluate remembering and forgetting evolving personal information. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Simulated personalities and LLM judges limit external validity. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Simulated personas and restricted user scope; efficiency/runtime not the principal evaluated outcome.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

LLM-judge agreement is not ground truth. Conditional failure samples cannot establish overall stale-memory frequency. Recommendation criteria may admit several valid responses.

## Relationship to Other Papers

PersonaMem studies evolving profiles; STALE and StateMem add explicit conflict/dependency probes.

## Relevance to EvoMem

A required counterexample/baseline when considering evaluate remembering and forgetting evolving personal information. Any proposal must state its additional assumption, test and advantage over this work.

## Does It Threaten Our Proposed Novelty?

**High for broad lifecycle/validity claims.** Specific overlap and residual hypotheses are assessed in [the novelty threat matrix](../../novelty_threat_matrix.md); being a strong baseline is not proof that every narrower question is closed.
