# Citation

**TRUSTMEM: Learning Trustworthy Memory Consolidation for LLM Agents with Long-Term Memory**. Yang, Tianyu; Paul, Sudipta; Srinivasan, Vijay; Kulkarni, Vivek; Chappidi, Srinivas. First public: 2026-06-23; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25161v1); v1 (2026-06-23).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Learn trustworthy memory consolidation with transition feedback

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Learn trustworthy memory consolidation with transition feedback. Its scope matters because relative verifier preference is not an absolute risk guarantee.

## Method

A frozen transition verifier assesses coverage, preservation and faithfulness of memory edits. Transition-ranked GRPO combines this preference signal with QA, compression, format and semantic feedback.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Learned construction
- **Retrieve:** Fixed evaluation retrieval
- **Update:** Learned memory editing
- **Consolidate:** Transition-verified consolidation
- **Invalidate:** Implicit through edit actions, not a formal invalidation algebra
- **Forget:** Editing/deletion under training reward

## Representation

Core, semantic and episodic state

## Controller / Decision Policy

Transition-ranked GRPO. RL construction training plus frozen-verifier calls. Exact comparable total GPU-hours not established here; heavier than a small classifier.

## Datasets

562 selected training instances from the Mem-alpha pool; 463 validation; three MemoryAgentBench capability groups and HaluMem.

## Baselines

Mem-alpha and prompted/learned construction systems in matched settings.

## Metrics

QA F1, omission, corruption, hallucination and compression.

## Main Findings

Consolidation fidelity already receives explicit learning signals and evaluation. Reported improvements over Mem-alpha include 12.14 F1 points in the stated aggregate; this does not establish full selective-forgetting coverage.

## Ablations

Action reward, verifier and transition ranking; removing key components materially lowers reported scores.

## What It Actually Solves

Learn trustworthy memory consolidation with transition feedback. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Relative verifier preference is not an absolute risk guarantee. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Multimodal and broader deployment generalization remain limitations/future work.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Verifier ranking is relative rather than a calibrated probability of edit correctness. Small hallucination rates judged by another LLM are not proof of zero false generalizations.

## Relationship to Other Papers

Mem-alpha supplies construction setting; HaluMem measures operation errors; OAS studies budget-dependent harm.

## Relevance to EvoMem
