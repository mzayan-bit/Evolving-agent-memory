# Citation

**MemSecBench: Tracking Agent Memory Poisoning from Persistence to Consequence and Repair**. Chen, Xuanze; Xie, Xukang; Fu, Wentao; Zhou, Jiajun; Yu, Shanqing; Xuan, Qi. First public: 2026-07-29; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080v1); v1 (2026-07-29).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Track poisoning through persistence, use, consequence and repair

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Track poisoning through persistence, use, consequence and repair. Its scope matters because benign preservation during repair is already measured.

## Method

Track an attack through stored persistence, retrieval exposure, adoption and external consequence. From the same poisoned state, execute a repair/forget branch and measure both removal and preservation of useful memory.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Attack reaches write path
- **Retrieve:** Exposure and adoption checkpoints
- **Update:** Repair branch
- **Consolidate:** Backend-specific transformation included
- **Invalidate:** Repair/removal checkpoints
- **Forget:** Measure removal and benign preservation separately

## Representation

Persistent memory attack/repair tasks

## Controller / Decision Policy

Checkpointed lifecycle evaluation. 24 API/backend compositions can be expensive; reproduce a small fixed composition first.

## Datasets

310 cases across 48 contexts; two harnesses, four backends and three models yield 24 configurations.

## Baselines

Backend/harness/model compositions and repair strategies.

## Metrics

Memory poisoning success, end-to-end success, conditional realization, removal and benign preservation.

## Main Findings

Reported macro persistence is 84.2%, end-to-end consequence 50.3%, repair removal 86.3% and benign preservation 62.5%. These distinct denominators must not be merged into one security score.

## Ablations

Composition and lifecycle checkpoint analyses; backend rankings reverse across harness/model settings.

## What It Actually Solves

Track poisoning through persistence, use, consequence and repair. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Benign preservation during repair is already measured. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Controlled attacks and finite system combinations cannot establish deployment-wide security.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Benign preservation is already a metric; G1 cannot claim inventing it. A repair that deletes everything can score high removal while failing utility. Conditional success requires its denominator.

## Relationship to Other Papers
