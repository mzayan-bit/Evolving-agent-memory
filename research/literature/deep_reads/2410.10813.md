# Citation

**LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory**. Wu, Di; Wang, Hongwei; Yu, Wenhao; Zhang, Yuwei; Chang, Kai-Wei; Yu, Dong. First public: 2024-10-14; Author-reported status: ICLR 2025. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813v2); v2 (2025-03-04).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Evaluate five long-term interactive memory abilities

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Evaluate five long-term interactive memory abilities. Its scope matters because oracle evidence and full-history settings must not be compared as equivalent.

## Method

Construct 500 questions testing information extraction, multi-session reasoning, knowledge updates, temporal reasoning and abstention. Scale histories while retaining evidence annotations, and analyze indexing, retrieval and reading separately.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Index history at different granularities
- **Retrieve:** Evaluate evidence retrieval and reading
- **Update:** Knowledge-update probes
- **Consolidate:** Compare compression/indexing designs
- **Invalidate:** Knowledge-update/abstention tasks, not executable lifecycle traces
- **Forget:** No comprehensive deletion-policy benchmark

## Representation

Scalable chat histories with evidence annotations

## Controller / Decision Policy

Indexing, retrieval and reading decomposition. Scales strongly with history length; API evaluation can be staged using S and oracle subsets.

## Datasets

LongMemEval-S approximately 115K tokens/history; LongMemEval-M approximately 1.5M; oracle-evidence condition distinct.

## Baselines

Commercial assistants, long-context models and retrieval-augmented memory configurations.

## Metrics

Question accuracy with model judging; evidence recall/NDCG; human agreement checks.

## Main Findings

Long-term memory is broader than literal recall even in this earlier benchmark. Perfect evidence retrieval can still leave reader errors; lossy summaries can omit important details.

## Ablations

Session/round segmentation, key expansion, time-aware filtering and structured chain-of-note reading.

## What It Actually Solves

Evaluate five long-term interactive memory abilities. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Oracle evidence and full-history settings must not be compared as equivalent. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Synthetic/scalable history construction and automated grading require care; benchmark does not represent every deployment.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Static post-hoc questions can test updates without exercising an online agent’s executed write/delete decisions. Oracle history access is an information advantage, not a deployable method.

## Relationship to Other Papers

LongMemEval-V2 is a later distinct benchmark; HaluMem and MemOps add operation-specific diagnosis.

## Relevance to EvoMem
