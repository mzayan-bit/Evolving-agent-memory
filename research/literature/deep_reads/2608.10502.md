**From Faulty Memories to Corrected Actions: Dependency-Guided Rollback Repair for Memory-Augmented Agents**. Yu, Caili; Wang, Yiqi; Zhang, Jiaqi; Duan, Yiqun; Zheng, Mingkai; Wu, Zhangkai; Shi, Kaize; Cai, Taotao. First public: 2026-08-11; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. ## Research Problem

Repair memory and actions while retaining unaffected work

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Repair memory and actions while retaining unaffected work. Its scope matters because diagnosed faulty ids and recorded dependency edges are assumed.

## Method

Given diagnosed faulty record IDs, construct a typed memory-to-action graph from runtime provenance. Traverse explicit descendants, retain records with independent trusted support, deactivate unsupported state and replay answer-relevant affected computation.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Track derived writes and runtime provenance
- **Retrieve:** Retrieve evidence during replay
- **Update:** Repair affected state
- **Consolidate:** Derived records can be reconsidered
- **Invalidate:** Dependency-guided deactivation
- **Forget:** Remove unsupported influence while retaining independent support

## Representation

Typed runtime dependency graph

## Controller / Decision Policy

Deactivate unsupported descendants and selectively replay. GPT-4o main setting and frozen-model replay; API-feasible with a restricted pilot.

## Datasets

150 selected injected-failure cases across three domains/four fault types; 50 adapted LongMemEval-V2 cases.

## Baselines

Six recovery strategies including deletion, reset/full replay and local repair variants.

## Metrics

Recovery, benign preservation, recurrence and replay cost.

## Main Findings

Reports 85.3% recovery versus 77.3% for the strongest compared alternative, with 100% benign preservation on the selected main set. Recurrence remains 26.6%; the problem is not solved completely.

## Ablations

Graph/dependency, support preservation and replay variants; further backbones in Appendix F.

## What It Actually Solves

Repair memory and actions while retaining unaffected work. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Diagnosed faulty IDs and recorded dependency edges are assumed. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Fault diagnosis is supplied; missing dependency edges are outside the recovery guarantee. Irreversible actions need environment reset or compensation.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Cases are selected where injection causes an error and downstream effects, so rates are conditional. Single temperature-zero runs do not remove stochastic or model-version uncertainty. Strong baseline for G1, not a novelty-free implementation template.

## Relationship to Other Papers

MemAudit diagnoses causes; PlanFence prevents stale action; execution-state unlearning gives a replay contract.

## Relevance to EvoMem
