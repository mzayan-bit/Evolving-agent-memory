**STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?**. Chao, Hanxiang; Bai, Yihan; Sheng, Rui; Li, Tianle; Sun, Yushi. First public: 2026-05-07; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527v1); v1 (2026-05-07).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Measure implicit invalidation and resistance to stale premises

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Measure implicit invalidation and resistance to stale premises. Its scope matters because single-conflict construction does not settle repeated noisy dependency repair.

## Method

Build single-attribute and dependency-mediated conflicts, then probe current-state resolution, resistance to stale premises and implicit policy adaptation. CUPMem represents active, stale and unknown-current state and uses propagation-aware search.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Structured state consolidation
- **Retrieve:** Propagation-aware search
- **Update:** Write-time adjudication
- **Consolidate:** Structured consolidation
- **Invalidate:** Active/stale/unknown-current transitions
- **Forget:** Semantic suppression of outdated state

## Representation

Dynamic scenarios; structured active/stale/unknown state

## Controller / Decision Policy

State adjudication and propagation-aware search. API inference over long histories; start with shorter matched subsets before full 150K histories.

## Datasets

400 expert-validated scenarios, 1,200 queries, 50-session histories up to approximately 150K tokens.

## Baselines

Long-context models and multiple agent-memory frameworks; CUPMem prototype.

## Metrics

State Resolution, Premise Resistance and Implicit Policy Adaptation.

## Main Findings

Dependency-sensitive revision already has a benchmark and baseline. Best reported evaluated model reaches 55.2%; many tested memory frameworks are below 10%, under the paper’s setup and versions.

## Ablations

Evidence accessibility versus adoption; diagnostic attention analysis; CUPMem components in appendix.

## What It Actually Solves

Measure implicit invalidation and resistance to stale premises. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Single-conflict construction does not settle repeated noisy dependency repair. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Synthetic scenario construction and one principal conflict pair limit complex repeated revision.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Attention patterns are diagnostic, not causal proof. Typed schema and explicit benchmark construction can favor the proposed solution. Repeated updates with ambiguous, missing lineage remain a different test.

## Relationship to Other Papers

StateAuditor repairs implicit response premises; StateMem tracks explicit relational dependencies.

## Relevance to EvoMem
