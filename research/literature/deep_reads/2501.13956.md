# Citation

**Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent Memory**. Rasmussen, Preston; Paliychuk, Pavlo; Beauvais, Travis; Ryan, Jack; Chalef, Daniel. First public: 2025-01-20; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956v1); v1 (2025-01-20).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Maintain evolving relational facts with temporal provenance

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Maintain evolving relational facts with temporal provenance. Its scope matters because temporal edge invalidation is not general downstream belief repair.

## Method

Episodes feed entity and semantic-edge extraction; community summaries organize broader context. Event-time validity and ingestion-time information coexist. Conflicting edges can expire without erasing their historical record.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Extract facts from episodes
- **Retrieve:** Cosine, BM25 and graph traversal; fusion and reranking
- **Update:** Invalidate conflicting edges and insert new facts
- **Consolidate:** Community summaries
- **Invalidate:** Temporal invalidation of conflicting edges
- **Forget:** Historical edges retained; not equivalent to physical deletion

## Representation

Bitemporal knowledge graph plus episodes

## Controller / Decision Policy

Hybrid graph, lexical and semantic retrieval. API LLM extraction/answering plus embeddings, graph infrastructure and reranking; full reproducible dollar total not established here.

## Datasets

Deep Memory Retrieval (500 conversations, five sessions); LongMemEval-S (approximately 115K tokens per history).

## Baselines

MemGPT, full context and retrieval-based configurations under the paper’s model-specific protocols.

## Metrics

Answer accuracy, judge scores, context tokens and latency.

## Main Findings

Demonstrates temporal graph memory on conversational QA. DMR is near saturation for some full-context settings, so improvements there do not settle hard lifecycle management. Later temporal-memory proposals cannot claim timestamped historical retention is new.

## Ablations

Retrieval and context configurations are compared; a controlled missing-dependency cascade experiment was not found.

## What It Actually Solves

Maintain evolving relational facts with temporal provenance. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Temporal edge invalidation is not general downstream belief repair. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Conclusion motivates further applications and retrieval improvements; no separately quantified guarantee of complete semantic conflict detection.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Entity/edge extraction and contradiction adjudication remain fallible. Temporal conflict on a fact is not a proof that all derived beliefs or pending actions have been repaired. Benchmark comparisons use particular answerer/judge choices.

## Relationship to Other Papers

TOKI, MemStrata, MemTX and StateMem make narrower temporal/revision semantics explicit.

## Relevance to EvoMem

A required counterexample/baseline when considering maintain evolving relational facts with temporal provenance. Any proposal must state its additional assumption, test and advantage over this work.
