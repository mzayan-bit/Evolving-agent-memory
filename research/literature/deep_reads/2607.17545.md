# Citation

**Retain or Consolidate? Budget-Dependent Operator Selection for Language Agent Memory**. Kang, Qingcan; Liu, Mingyang; Kai, Shixiong; Liang, Kaichao; Tang, Zhentao; Cui, Yuqi; Zhong, Tao; Yuan, Mingxuan. First public: 2026-07-20; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17545v2); v2 (2026-07-21).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Choose retention versus consolidation under a budget

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Choose retention versus consolidation under a budget. Its scope matters because query-conditioned one-shot utility differs from future revision utility.

## Method

Score retain, merge, abstract and rewrite operators using pre-generation features and learned utility/risk estimates. Utility separates evidence coverage from replacement harm, and budget determines whether compression is useful.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Operates on candidate memory
- **Retrieve:** Query/evidence-conditioned selection
- **Update:** Replacement by a transformed representation
- **Consolidate:** Explicit operator selection
- **Invalidate:** Avoid harmful replacement empirically
- **Forget:** Budgeted omission, not general semantic deletion

## Representation

Raw and transformed memory candidates

## Controller / Decision Policy

Learned operator utility and empirical risk control. Lightweight ridge heads and frozen LLM transformations; feasible, but offline candidate-label generation has API cost.

## Datasets

LongMemEval: 350/75/75 splits and grouped folds; budgets of 32/64/128/256 tokens; additional full-history non-oracle check.

## Baselines

Retain, fixed merge, other fixed operators and learned/risk-aware routers.

## Metrics

Answer utility, harmful replacement, token budget and risk/coverage tradeoffs.

## Main Findings

Retaining evidence can be best at looser budgets. Main oracle-evidence improvements must not be transferred to non-oracle retrieval: the appendix reports fixed merge 45.7 versus ridge 44.7 and safer router 39.3 in one full-history test.

## Ablations

Operator, features, evidence coverage/replacement components, budget and risk threshold.

## What It Actually Solves

Choose retention versus consolidation under a budget. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Query-conditioned one-shot utility differs from future revision utility. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Joint persistent retrieval/packing and broader generalization remain further work.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Calibration is empirical and distribution-dependent, not a population safety certificate. Query-known compression does not test whether discarded evidence would be needed for an unknown later correction.
