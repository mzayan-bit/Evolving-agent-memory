# Citation

**Evaluating Memory in LLM Agents via Incremental Multi-Turn Interactions**. Hu, Yuanzhe; Wang, Yu; McAuley, Julian. First public: 2025-07-07; arXiv preprint; no publication venue independently verified. [Reviewed primary version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257v4); v4 (2026-06-28).

Evidence level: targeted deep reading of method, evaluation and relevant limitations/appendices; no independent experimental reproduction. Author reports are distinguished below from reviewer inferences.

## Research Problem

Evaluate incremental memory ingestion and four capabilities

## Why This Paper Exists

The design targets a concrete weakness in how prior agents evaluate or use memory. Evaluate incremental memory ingestion and four capabilities. Its scope matters because chunk sizes and benchmark revisions affect fair comparison.

## Method

Feed long source material incrementally in chunks, then test accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding and selective forgetting. Fact-consolidation streams introduce replacements and multi-hop relations.

## Memory Lifecycle Covered

- **Write:** Incremental ingestion
- **Retrieve:** Accurate-retrieval tasks
- **Update:** Counterfactual fact replacements
- **Consolidate:** Long-range integration/learning probes
- **Invalidate:** Selective-forgetting capability
- **Forget:** Explicit selective-forgetting evaluation

## Representation

Chunked interaction streams

## Controller / Decision Policy

Retrieval, learning, understanding and selective forgetting tasks. Large histories and per-chunk writes can be costly; fix ingestion tokens and calls in comparisons.

## Datasets

MemoryAgentBench v4: multiple source datasets; chunk sizes 512 and 4,096 tokens are used under differing method budgets.

## Baselines

Long-context models, lexical/dense/graph RAG and commercial or managed memory systems.

## Metrics

Task-specific exact/F1/accuracy measures and cost/performance analyses.

## Main Findings

The benchmark already includes dynamic replacement and selective forgetting. Older papers call the relevant split Conflict Resolution; the inspected v4 taxonomy names Selective Forgetting. Preserve version and split names.

## Ablations

Chunk size, context and system comparisons; appendix documents constituent datasets and efficiency.

## What It Actually Solves

Evaluate incremental memory ingestion and four capabilities. The empirical support is restricted to the tasks and controls described above.

## What It Does NOT Solve

Chunk sizes and benchmark revisions affect fair comparison. This is a scope assessment, not a claim that the approach could never be extended.

## Author-Stated Limitations

Task conversion and budget choices constrain conclusions about unconstrained real agents.

## Additional Limitations We Observe

Unequal chunk sizes change write-call counts and information granularity. Scores from three selected capability families cannot be reported as full benchmark coverage.
